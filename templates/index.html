Why are these close but not identical? Black-Scholes solves the pricing equation analytically — it's exact under its assumptions. Monte Carlo simulates thousands of random paths and averages payoffs — a statistical estimate. Both converge to the same true price; the gap shrinks as simulation count increases. Option Greeks

Δ
Delta
—
Option price change per $1 move in the stock. A call with Δ=0.55 gains $0.55 when the stock rises $1.
Γ
Gamma
—
How fast Delta itself changes. High Γ means the option accelerates — Delta moves quickly as the stock moves.
Θ
Theta
—
Value lost per calendar day just from time passing. Options are wasting assets — every day costs a little.
ν
Vega
—
Price change per 1% move in volatility. Both calls and puts gain when vol rises — uncertainty has value.
ρ
Rho
—
Price change per 1% shift in interest rates. Calls benefit from higher rates; puts are hurt by them.

Exotic Option Pricing

Beyond vanilla — these path-dependent derivatives are priced by Monte Carlo only. Each modifies the payoff rule in a different way, producing a different price profile.

Baseline
Vanilla Call (BS)
—
The classic: a right to buy at K. Black-Scholes gives the exact analytical price. All other exotics are compared against this baseline.
Stochastic
Monte Carlo Est.
—
The same vanilla call priced by simulation. The small gap from Black-Scholes is statistical noise — it shrinks with more simulations.
Path-Dependent
Asian Option
—
Payoff is based on the average stock price over the option's life. Averaging smooths volatility — Asian options are cheaper than vanilla.
Knock-Out
Barrier Option
—
If the stock ever touches the barrier, the option instantly expires worthless. This knock-out risk makes barrier options cheaper than vanilla.
Retrospective
Lookback Option
—
Payoff = final price minus the stock's historical minimum. This perfect hindsight premium makes it the most expensive option here.
